Planning Your Window Replacement Project

Upgrading your windows improves energy efficiency, reduces noise, and boosts curb appeal. However, prices can fluctuate drastically depending on the materials and installation methods.

Retrofit vs. Full-Frame Installation

  • Retrofit (Insert) Windows: The most common and affordable method. The contractor leaves your existing window frame and exterior trim intact, inserting the new window into the old opening. This is calculated in our standard pricing above.
  • Full-Frame Replacement: Requires stripping the window down to the house framing (studs). This is necessary if there is rot or if you are changing the size of the window. Expect to add $150 to $300 per window for the extra labor and exterior trim work.

Energy Efficiency Upgrades

When selecting your windows, look for the Energy Star label. Upgrading from double-pane to triple-pane glass, or adding Argon gas fills and Low-E (low-emissivity) coatings will increase the upfront cost but maximize your savings on heating and cooling bills over time.
