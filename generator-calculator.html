Understanding Your Standby Generator Quote

A whole-house standby generator permanently monitors your utility power 24/7. Within 10 seconds of a grid blackout, the automatic transfer switch starts the generator and safely transfers your home's electrical circuits, keeping refrigeration, air conditioning, and medical devices operational.

4 Generator Contractor Red Flags to Watch For

  • Undersizing the Natural Gas Pipe Supply: A 22kW generator requires 250,000 to 290,000 BTUs of fuel per hour at full load. Plumbers who tie into an existing 1/2-inch or 3/4-inch branch line cause massive gas pressure drops, stalling the generator the moment the home furnace or water heater ignites.
  • Violating NFPA 37 Setback Clearance Distances: Building codes strictly require generators to sit at least 5 feet from operable windows, exterior doors, and soffit vents, and at least 18 inches from non-combustible exterior walls. Violating setbacks risks deadly carbon monoxide gas entering living spaces.
  • Skipping Municipal Electrical & Gas Utility Permits: Generators involve high-voltage utility transfer switches and gas pressure regulators. Unpermitted installations violate municipal building codes and utility interconnection rules, creating severe liability if grid backfeed injures utility workers.
  • Selling Expensive Liquid-Cooled Commercial Units for Normal Homes: High-commission sales reps often push $22,000+ commercial automotive liquid-cooled units on homes under 4,500 sq ft. Modern air-cooled 24kW–26kW units deliver whole-home power for less than half the investment.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does a standby generator turn on automatically during a power outage?

Yes. The automatic transfer switch (ATS) detects utility voltage loss and signals the generator engine to start within 10 to 12 seconds. When grid utility power is restored, the system automatically transfers back and shuts down the generator after a cool-down cycle.

How often does a standby generator require maintenance?

Generators perform a weekly 5-minute self-test exercise. Oil and filter changes are recommended every 100 to 200 operating hours or once annually, typically costing $200 to $350 for an annual dealer maintenance plan.
