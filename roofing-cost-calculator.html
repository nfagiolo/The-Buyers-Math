Understanding Your Roofing Estimate

When planning a roof replacement, several variables impact your final out-of-pocket expense. This calculator uses baseline industry averages to provide a realistic starting budget, but your exact quote will depend on the specifics of your home and chosen materials.

Key Variables in This Calculator

  • Square Footage (Roof Size): Roofing materials and labor are calculated by the "square," which equals 100 square feet of roof area. Larger footprints naturally require more materials and extended labor hours.
  • Material Type: Standard 3-tab asphalt shingles are the most budget-friendly option. Upgrading to architectural shingles, standing seam metal, or slate significantly increases the upfront material cost but drastically extends the lifespan of the roof.
  • Roof Pitch (Steepness): A steeper roof is more difficult to walk on. High-pitch roofs require specialized safety harnesses, scaffolding, and slower, more meticulous labor, which drives up the installation price.
  • Tear-Off and Disposal: Removing the existing roof involves intensive manual labor and heavy disposal fees for the old materials.

Additional Factors Influencing Final Costs

  • Decking Repairs: The plywood decking beneath your shingles must be structurally sound. If your contractor discovers water damage or wood rot during the tear-off, replacing those sheets will add unexpected material and labor costs.
  • Ventilation and Underlayment: Upgrading to high-quality synthetic underlayment, ice-and-water shields, and ridge vents will add to the initial quote but protect your investment against moisture damage and voided warranties.
  • Local Labor Rates: Contractor fees fluctuate based on your region's cost of living, seasonality, and local demand following severe weather events.

Frequently Asked Questions

  • What is a roofing square? A roofing square is a standard construction measurement representing a 10x10 foot area, or 100 square feet of roof surface.
  • Can I install new shingles over my old roof? While "roofing over" (a layover) saves money on tear-off labor and dumpster fees, it adds extreme weight to your home's structural framing and traps heat, which often voids the new shingles' manufacturer warranty.
  • How long does a new roof last? Standard asphalt shingles generally last 15 to 25 years. Architectural asphalt can last up to 30 years, while metal and slate roofs can easily exceed 50 years with proper maintenance.
