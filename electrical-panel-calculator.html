2026 Electrical Panel Heavy-Up Cost & NEC 2023 Compliance Guide

Upgrading a legacy 100-amp or 150-amp electrical service panel (including recalled Federal Pacific Stab-Lok, Zinsco, Challenger, or split-bus panels) to a modern 200-amp, 40-space copper-bus load center is the foundational step for home electrification. Under the 2023 National Electrical Code (NEC), a full service replacement triggers three mandatory safety upgrades that every homeowner must verify on contractor proposals: an Exterior Emergency Service Disconnect (NEC 230.85), a Type 1 or Type 2 Whole-Home Surge Protective Device (NEC 230.67), and Interconnected Dual Grounding Electrodes bonded to incoming metallic water piping (NEC 250.53).

Line-Item Cost Breakdown by Service Tier

  • 100A Subpanel Addition ($1,150 – $1,850): Ideal when your existing main breaker is already rated for 200A and passes an NEC Article 220 load calculation, but physical breaker spaces are full.
  • 200A Panel Swap Only ($2,100 – $2,950): Replaces an outdated indoor 200A breaker box while reusing compliant 2/0 copper or 4/0 aluminum service entrance conductors and an existing 200A meter base.
  • 100A-to-200A Full Service Heavy-Up ($3,200 – $4,600): Complete replacement from the utility weatherhead down—including a new 200A outdoor meter-main combo, 2/0 copper riser, 40-space indoor load center, dual 8-ft copper ground rods, and whole-home SPD.
  • 200A-to-400A Class 320 Dual-Panel Upgrade ($5,800 – $9,200): Required for large all-electric estates running dual heat pumps, multiple 48A EV chargers, and electric tankless water heaters. Frequently requires utility transformer capacity review and underground lateral trenching.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does my utility company charge to disconnect power during a panel upgrade?

Most electric utilities perform standard overhead service drop disconnects and reconnects at zero direct fee or a nominal $150–$350 scheduling fee, provided your licensed electrician files an approved municipal electrical cut-in card.

Can I install a 48A EV charger on a 100A panel without upgrading to 200A?

Only if you install an UL-listed Automatic Energy Management / EVEMS load-shedding device (NEC 625.42) that throttles or pauses EV charging whenever household oven, dryer, or HVAC loads peak—otherwise a 200A heavy-up is required.
