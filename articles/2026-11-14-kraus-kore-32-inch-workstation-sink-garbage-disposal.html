Licensed Trade Labor Breakdown (2026 Rates)

While assembling tubular slip-joint P-traps is approachable for an experienced DIYer, modifying in-wall waste lines, cutting quartz countertops, and pulling dedicated electrical circuits require licensed tradespeople. Below are the prevailing 2026 national average labor costs broken down by specialty trade:

  • Licensed Master/Journeyman Plumber ($115 to $185 per hour): A straightforward swap with existing compatible rough-in plumbing takes 2.5 to 3.5 hours ($350 to $650 total). However, because the Kraus Kore sink is 10 inches deep and the disposal outlet sits roughly 6 inches below the sink flange, the horizontal waste arm must enter the wall no higher than 14 inches from the cabinet floor. If your existing drain stub-out sits at 16 to 18 inches, the plumber must open the cabinet back, cut the vertical 2-inch DWV stack, and lower the sanitary tee. This rough-in modification adds $350 to $600 in labor.
  • Licensed Electrician ($95 to $155 per hour): If your kitchen lacks an under-sink receptacle for the disposal, an electrician will charge $250 to $480 to snake a new 14/2 or 12/2 NM-B Romex cable from the main breaker panel (or an permissible adjacent junction), install a surface-mounted handy box, and wire a dual-function AFCI/GFCI receptacle.
  • Countertop Fabricator / Stone Mason ($200 to $450 flat trip fee): Retrofitting a 32-inch Kraus undermount sink into an existing 30-inch cutout requires diamond-blade grinding, polishing on-site with wet pads, and drilling two 1-3/8" holes for the faucet and air switch button. Expect a minimum 2-hour shop dispatch charge.

Plumbing & Electrical Code Compliance (IRC / IPC / NEC)

Unpermitted or non-compliant plumbing modifications under a kitchen sink routinely get flagged by home inspectors during real estate transactions and can void homeowner insurance policies in the event of water damage. Ensure your project adheres to the following 2026 building standards:

IRC & IPC Plumbing Rules

  • Sanitary Tee Orientation (IPC Section 706.3): When connecting the horizontal trap arm to the vertical drain stack inside the wall, you must use a sanitary tee. Using a Wye fitting on a horizontal-to-vertical transition without a dedicated re-vent creates an illegal S-trap configuration, which will siphon your P-trap dry and allow sewer gas into the kitchen.
  • Air Admittance Valves (IRC P3114): In island sink configurations where a traditional atmospheric vent stack cannot be run without a costly island loop vent, local jurisdictions generally permit an Oatey Studor Redi-Vent (AAV). The AAV must be installed at least 4 inches above the horizontal branch drain and remain accessible for replacement—never sealed behind drywall.
  • Baffled Tee & Directional Flow: If forgoing a garbage disposal on a dual-drain setup, a baffled end-outlet continuous waste tee is required. With a single-bowl Kraus workstation sink and a disposal, the disposal acts as the sole drain receptor; ensure the dishwasher drain hose utilizes a high loop strapped to the underside of the countertop or a deck-mounted air gap per local municipal amendments.

NEC Electrical Requirements

  • GFCI and AFCI Protection (NEC 210.8(A) & 210.12): Under the latest National Electrical Code cycles enforced in 2026, any 120V, 15A or 20A receptacle installed within 6 feet of the top inside edge of the sink bowl requires both Ground-Fault Circuit-Interrupter (GFCI) and Arc-Fault Circuit-Interrupter (AFCI) protection.
  • Cord-and-Plug Disconnecting Means (NEC 422.16(B)(1)): Hardwiring a residential garbage disposal directly with armored cable is largely obsolete. Code allows—and inspectors prefer—a flexible 3-prong appliance cord between 18 inches and 36 inches in length plugged into an accessible under-sink receptacle. This serves as a visible safety disconnect during maintenance.

2026 Federal Tax Credits (25C / 25D / 30C) & Rebate Eligibility

Homeowners frequently ask whether high-efficiency kitchen plumbing upgrades qualify for federal incentives under the Inflation Reduction Act (IRA) in 2026. Directly speaking, stainless steel workstation sinks, garbage disposals, and plumbing rough-in piping do not qualify for Section 25C (Energy Efficient Home Improvement Credit), Section 25D (Residential Clean Energy Credit), or Section 30C (Alternative Fuel Vehicle Refueling Property Credit).

Section 25C strictly covers the building envelope, heat pumps, biomass stoves, and upgraded electrical panels (up to $600 when paired with a qualified energy property upgrade). However, you can still capture indirect rebates and utility savings in two ways:

  • EPA WaterSense Municipal Rebates: By pairing your Kraus Kore sink with a WaterSense-certified kitchen faucet (flow rate capped at 1.5 to 1.8 GPM) and an ENERGY STAR-certified dishwasher during the remodel, you may qualify for $50 to $150 in direct local water authority bill credits.
  • Point-of-Use Heat Pump or Tankless Water Heater Integration: If your kitchen sits far from your primary water heater and you install a qualified ENERGY STAR heat pump water heater (or upgrade a subpanel under 25C to support broader electrification), those specific mechanical components are eligible for a 30% federal tax credit up to $2,000.

Exact Installation Preparation Steps

Whether you are performing the rough-in yourself or prepping the workspace to minimize your plumber’s billable hours, follow this exact sequence before the new hardware arrives:

  • Step 1: Verify Base Cabinet Clearances: Measure the interior width of your sink base cabinet. The Kraus KWF210-33 apron-front or KWU110-32 undermount requires a true 36-inch interior cabinet width. If retrofitting into a 33-inch cabinet, you must step down to the Kraus KWU110-30 (30-inch) model, or be prepared to notch the top 2 inches of the plywood cabinet sidewalls.
  • Step 2: Map the Drain Centerline Elevation: Measure from the floor of the cabinet up to the center of the existing drain pipe coming out of the wall. Next, measure from the top of the countertop down 16.5 inches (10-inch sink depth + 0.5-inch countertop overhang + 6-inch disposal discharge drop). If your wall pipe is higher than this mark, water will pool inside the garbage disposal grinding chamber and rust the armature. Open the back of the cabinet now so the plumber has immediate access to the vertical stack.
  • Step 3: Decommission and Clear the Bay: Shut off the hot and cold supply stops (or the main house water shutoff if replacing the angle stops). Disconnect the dishwasher drain line, unplug the old disposal, and detach the P-trap. Remove the cabinet center stile temporarily (if present) and lay down a 1/4-inch waterproof PVC cabinet mat over the base floor before installing new plumbing.
  • Step 4: Pre-Assemble on the Sawhorses: Never install the disposal mounting flange or faucet after the heavy sink is mounted under the stone countertop. Flip the Kraus sink upside down on a padded workbench, apply plumber's putty (or use the included neoprene gasket for stainless steel), and lock the InSinkErator Quick Lock mount and faucet shank in place while you have full 360-degree visibility.
