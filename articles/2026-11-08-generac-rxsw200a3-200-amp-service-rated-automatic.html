Because it is service-rated (equipped with an integrated 200-amp main utility circuit breaker), it becomes your home's primary service disconnect under modern electrical codes, dictating strict utility coordination, heavy-gauge wire routing, and licensed master electrician labor.

Licensed Trade Labor & Utility Coordination Costs

Installing a service-rated automatic transfer switch is substantially more labor-intensive than installing a non-service-rated switch or a manual interlock kit. Because the RXSW200A3 sits directly between the utility meter pan and your existing main breaker panel, the utility company must disconnect power at the transformer or pull the meter before work begins.

  • Master & Journeyman Electrician Hourly Rates: Expect to pay $115 to $185 per hour in 2026 for a licensed electrical contractor, or $165 to $240 per hour for a two-person crew (one Journeyman, one Apprentice).
  • Standard Labor Hours (8 to 12 Hours Total): A standard back-to-back installation—where the utility meter is on the exterior wall directly opposite the indoor main panel—requires 8 to 10 man-hours. This puts baseline trade labor between $1,150 and $1,950.
  • Complex Routing & Sub-Panel Conversions (+$450 to +$900): Once the RXSW200A3 is installed upstream, your existing 200-amp main breaker panel legally becomes a downstream sub-panel. The electrician must spend 1.5 to 3 hours separating the neutral and ground conductors inside your existing panel, installing an isolated ground bar kit, and removing the main bonding jumper.
  • Utility Pull/Reconnect Fees: Depending on your regional electric utility, scheduling a meter pull, cut-in card inspection, and reconnection adds $150 to $350 in administrative and standby dispatch fees.

NEC Code, Permitting Rules, and 2026 Federal Tax Credits

All 200-amp automatic transfer switch installations must pass a municipal rough-in and final electrical inspection before the utility company will re-energize the meter.

NEC 2023/2026 & IRC Compliance Rules

  • NEC 230.85 (Emergency Disconnects): The RXSW200A3 satisfies the mandatory exterior emergency disconnect rule when mounted outdoors adjacent to the meter socket and properly labeled as "EMERGENCY DISCONNECT, SERVICE DISCONNECT".
  • NEC 250.24 & 250.32 (Neutral-to-Ground Bonding): Because the RXSW200A3 contains the first service overcurrent protection device (the 200A main breaker), the neutral and ground must be bonded exclusively inside the RXSW200A3. Downstream panels must have floating neutrals (4-wire feed) to prevent parallel neutral current paths on metallic conduits and grounding electrodes.
  • NEC 702.4 (Capacity and Load Shedding): When pairing a 200-amp ATS with an air-cooled standby generator (such as a Generac Guardian 22kW or 24kW), the NEC requires automatic load management if the generator cannot carry the full calculated load of the dwelling. The RXSW200A3 includes built-in control blocks to shed up to four 24VAC air conditioner contactors without additional modules.
  • Municipal Permit Fees: Electrical permits for a 200A service modification and transfer switch range from $125 to $350 nationwide.

2026 Federal Tax Credits (Section 25C & 25D Eligibility)

Homeowners frequently ask if the Generac RXSW200A3 qualifies for federal clean energy incentives. Under current 2026 IRS guidelines:

  • IRC Section 25C (Energy Efficient Home Improvement Credit): Section 25C offers a 30% tax credit (up to $600 maximum) for upgrading electrical panelboards, sub-panels, or feeder circuitry, provided the upgrade is installed in conjunction with another qualified energy property (such as a qualified heat pump, heat pump water heater, or central A/C) and has a load capacity of at least 200 amps. A standalone installation for a fossil-fuel standby generator does not qualify for 25C.
  • IRC Section 25D (Residential Clean Energy Credit): The 30% uncapped 25D credit applies only if the transfer switch is integrated as an essential balance-of-system component for a solar photovoltaic (PV) array or a battery energy storage system (BESS) of 3 kWh or greater.

Step-by-Step Installation Preparation & How to Save 20%–30%

Electrical contractors routinely apply a 25% to 40% supply-house markup on the Generac RXSW200A3 enclosure, SMM modules, and specialty composite generator cable to cover their procurement overhead and warranty liability. You can legally and safely reduce your total project expenditure by 20% to 30% ($500 to $1,100 in savings) by purchasing the hardware kit directly and hiring an independent licensed electrician on a Time and Materials (T&M) basis.

Pre-Installation Site Preparation Checklist

  1. Verify Utility Meter Architecture: Confirm your utility meter socket is a standalone 200-amp pan and not an "all-in-one" meter-main combo panel. If you have a meter-main combo, the RXSW200A3 cannot be inserted between the meter and the main busbar without replacing the entire meter base.
  2. Clear the 36" x 30" NEC 110.26 Working Space: The RXSW200A3 measures 30" H x 13.5" W x 6.3" D. Ensure the mounting wall has at least 30 inches of horizontal width and 36 inches of unobstructed front depth, free of gas meters, dryer vents, and shrubs.
  3. Pre-Order the Exact Material Bundle: Purchase the Generac RXSW200A3, required Generac 7000 Smart Management Modules (for electric ranges, dryers, or EV chargers), and the exact linear footage of 4/0 Aluminum SER cable and Schedule 80 PVC conduit prior to booking the electrician.
  4. Perform the Trenching Yourself: If the generator pad is located more than 5 feet from the transfer switch, dig the 18-inch-deep conduit trench yourself (after calling 811 for utility locates). Saving a licensed electrician from 3 hours of manual digging instantly trims $350 to $550 off your final labor invoice.
