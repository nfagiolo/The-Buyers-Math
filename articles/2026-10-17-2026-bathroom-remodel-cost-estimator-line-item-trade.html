How Contractor Markup Works and What Is Reasonable

Most general contractors apply a markup structure consisting of two distinct layers: overhead (typically 10–18% of direct costs) and profit (typically 8–15% of direct costs). Combined, a legitimate all-in contractor margin of 20–33% on a mid-range bathroom remodel is industry-standard. When itemized bids show markups exceeding 40% on material costs or subcontractor invoices, that gap warrants a direct audit conversation.

  • Material markup: Industry standard is 15–25% above supplier invoice price.
  • Subcontractor markup: GC markup on licensed sub labor runs 10–20%.
  • Lump-sum bids: Obscure markups; always request a cost-plus or itemized alternative for projects exceeding $15,000.
  • Change order inflation: Unscrupulous contractors average 35–50% markup on change order materials. Negotiate a change-order cap in the contract.

Licensed Trade Labor Rates by Discipline (2026 National Averages)

Labor is typically 40–55% of total bathroom remodel cost. The following rates reflect 2026 BLS median wages plus benefits burden (approximately 30% above base wage) for licensed, insured tradespeople in mid-cost metro areas. Rural markets run 15–25% lower; high-cost metros (San Francisco, New York, Boston) run 30–50% higher.

Plumbing

  • Licensed journeyman plumber: $98–$135/hour billed rate
  • Fixture rough-in (tub, toilet, sink): $680–$1,100 per fixture
  • Full bathroom rough-in and trim-out (new construction scope): $2,400–$4,200
  • Water heater upgrade to support expanded bathroom demand: $900–$1,800 installed

Electrical (NEC 2023/2026 Compliance)

The 2023 National Electrical Code—adopted by most jurisdictions by 2026—mandates GFCI protection for all receptacles within 6 feet of a sink (Article 210.8) and requires arc-fault circuit interrupter (AFCI) protection on all 120V, 15A and 20A circuits in dwelling units (Article 210.12). A dedicated 20A circuit for the bathroom is also required under NEC 210.11(C)(3).

  • Licensed electrician billed rate: $110–$155/hour
  • GFCI outlet installation (code-required): $180–$320 per outlet
  • Exhaust fan with humidity sensor (IRC Section R303.3 ventilation requirement): $380–$620 installed
  • Dedicated 20A bathroom circuit (often missing in pre-1990 homes): $450–$900
  • Heated floor thermostat wiring: $220–$480

Tile and Waterproofing

  • Tile setter labor (ceramic/porcelain floor): $12–$22/sq ft installed
  • Large-format tile (24"×24") labor premium: +$4–$8/sq ft
  • Schluter KERDI or RedGard waterproof membrane installation: $3.50–$6/sq ft labor
  • Shower niche fabrication: $280–$550 each

IRA Tax Credits Applicable to Bathroom Upgrades (2026)

The Inflation Reduction Act extends several credits through 2032 that intersect directly with bathroom renovation scope. Section 25C provides a 30% tax credit (up to $600/year) on qualifying water heaters—heat pump water heaters (HPWHs) qualify and carry an elevated cap of $2,000 under the appliance category. Section 25D covers residential clean energy equipment; solar thermal water heating systems qualify at 30% with no dollar cap through 2032, stepping down to 26% in 2033. Section 30C (Alternative Fuel Vehicle Refueling Property) does not apply to bathroom scope. Homeowners must file IRS Form 5695 and retain manufacturer certification statements to claim these credits.
