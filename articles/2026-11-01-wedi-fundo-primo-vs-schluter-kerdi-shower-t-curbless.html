Wedi Fundo Primo vs Schluter KERDI-SHOWER-T Curbless Shower Pan Installed Cost Guide

In 2026, the total installed cost for a curbless shower pan ranges from $2,650 to $5,800 depending on subfloor framing modifications, while DIY material kit costs range from $580 to $1,450 for a standard 48"x48" footprint. When engineering a zero-threshold (curbless) wet room, contractors and homeowners almost exclusively specify either the Wedi Fundo Primo system or the Schluter KERDI-SHOWER-T system. While Schluter dominates retail distribution with a lower upfront material price using expanded polystyrene (EPS) and sheet membranes, Wedi utilizes a closed-cell extruded polystyrene (XPS) core that eliminates tedious seam-taping and accelerates installation timelines. Below is an empirical breakdown of component pricing, trade labor rates, structural code compliance, and supply-house markup strategies for your bathroom takeoff.

Licensed Trade Labor Breakdown

While DIY material budgets favor Schluter, professional installation costs converge rapidly because Wedi requires fewer labor steps. Curbless showers are labor-intensive due to the structural requirement of recessing the pan flush with the adjacent bathroom floor. Expect the following 2026 licensed trade rates:

  • Structural Framing & Subfloor Modification ($650 – $1,250): A licensed carpenter ($85–$125/hr) must saw out the existing 3/4" OSB or plywood subfloor between the floor joists. They then install 2x4 or 2x6 cleats flush with the bottom of the joists and drop in new recessed 3/4" plywood flush with the top of the joists. Sistering joists may be required if old plumbing notched the structural lumber.
  • Licensed Plumbing Rough-In ($450 – $900): A licensed plumber ($110–$175/hr) must relocate the 2-inch P-trap and solvent-weld the drain body at the exact lowered elevation. Because the subfloor is dropped 3/4", the P-trap arm must be re-pitched to maintain a strict 1/4-inch per foot downward slope to the main stack.
  • Certified Tile Setter Labor ($1,550 – $3,650): Tile mechanics typically charge $25 to $45 per square foot for curbless wet-area preparation and waterproofing, or $95–$140/hr. Installing a Schluter curbless pan takes roughly 1.5 days due to thinset cure times and meticulous corner/band buildup (which can cause "sheetrock pillowing" under small mosaic tiles). Wedi installs in roughly 4 to 6 hours because the joint sealant cures rapidly and creates zero topological buildup at the wall-to-pan transition.

IRC Building Codes, Permits, and 2026 Federal Tax Credits

IRC & IPC Code Compliance

Every curbless shower conversion requires a municipal building and plumbing permit ($150 to $400 depending on jurisdiction) because you are altering the structural subfloor and waste plumbing. Inspectors enforce strict International Residential Code (IRC) and International Plumbing Code (IPC) mandates:

  • Joist Notching & Boring (IRC R502.8): You cannot arbitrarily notch floor joists to drop a shower pan. Notches in the middle third of a joist span are strictly prohibited, and notches at the ends cannot exceed one-fourth of the joist depth. If you have engineered I-joists or trusses, cutting the top flange without an engineered stamp will result in an immediate red-tag violation.
  • Drainage Slope & Flood Testing (IPC 417.5.2): The pan must maintain a minimum 1/4-inch per foot (2%) slope toward the drain. Furthermore, inspectors require a 24-hour flood test—plugging the drain with a pneumatic test ball and filling the pan with 2 inches of water—prior to setting a single tile. For curbless pans, contractors must build a temporary sacrificial dam using 2x4s and sealant to retain the test water.
  • Electrical Clearances (NEC 406.9(C)): Under the National Electrical Code, no receptacles, switches, or non-wet-rated luminaires can be installed within the shower zone or within 3 feet horizontally and 8 feet vertically of the curbless threshold edge.

2026 Federal Tax Credits & Deductions

Homeowners frequently ask if bathroom remodels qualify for federal green energy incentives. Standard bathroom waterproofing kits do not qualify for IRS Section 25C (Energy Efficient Home Improvement Credit), 25D (Residential Clean Energy), or 30C (EV Charger) tax credits. Those credits are strictly reserved for building envelope insulation, heat pump water heaters, and solar/EV infrastructure.

However, if you are installing a curbless Wedi or Schluter shower specifically for a household member with mobility impairments, Parkinson's, or age-related disability, the entire installed cost (materials and labor) may qualify as a Capital Expense Medical Deduction under IRS Publication 502. Because a zero-threshold shower is recognized as an accessibility modification that does not significantly inflate the appraisal value of the home, expenses exceeding 7.5% of your Adjusted Gross Income (AGI) can be written off on Schedule A if backed by a physician's letter of medical necessity.

Exact Installation Preparation Steps

Whether you hire a crew or tackle the preparation yourself, skipping a single substrate preparation step will void the manufacturer's 10-year (Schluter) or 10-to-20-year (Wedi) system warranty. Follow this exact sequence:

  1. Laser Level Assessment: Shoot a 360-degree cross-line laser across the existing bathroom floor. Wood joists crown and sag; your recessed subfloor pocket must be shimmed or ground completely dead-level in all directions before setting a pre-sloped foam pan.
  2. Subfloor Recess & Cleating: Cut the existing subfloor flush with the inner edges of the framing joists and studs using a toe-kick saw. Screw and construction-glue 2x4 ledger cleats along the sides of the joists, recessed exactly 3/4" from the top of the joist. Cut 3/4" exterior-grade CDX plywood or OSB panels to drop snugly between the joists on top of the cleats.
  3. Self-Leveling Underlayment (If Applicable): If the recessed plywood bays are out of plane by more than 1/8" over 10 feet, prime the wood and pour a feather-edge cementitious self-leveler before dry-fitting the foam pan.
  4. Mortar Bedding: Using a 1/4" x 3/8" square-notched trowel, comb modified thinset (Wedi) or Schluter ALL-SET onto the recessed plywood and the underside of the foam pan (back-buttering). Walk the pan into the mortar bed to collapse the ridges and eliminate hollow air voids that cause point-load cracking under heavy foot traffic or shower wheelchairs.
  5. Outer Bathroom Floor Transition: Extend your waterproofing across the rest of the bathroom floor using an uncoupling membrane (Schluter DITRA or Wedi Subliner Dry) sealed directly to the edge of the curbless pan. In a curbless setup, the entire bathroom effectively becomes the wet zone.

How to Save 20%–30% by Bypassing Contractor Supply-House Markup

When a general contractor or tile contractor bids a curbless bathroom, they purchase their Wedi or Schluter kits from regional wholesale distributors (such as Dal-Tile, Floor & Decor Pro, or local plumbing supply houses) at a contractor trade discount. However, contractors routinely mark up these material packages by 25% to 40% on your final invoice to cover procurement time, transport, and warranty overhead. On a $1,400 Wedi kit, that equates to $350 to $560 in pure markup before a single tool is lifted.

You can legally and strategically eliminate this surcharge by negotiating a "Labor and Rough Sundries Only" contract with your tile setter, while purchasing the exact SKU takeoff kit yourself through online wholesale portals or direct-to-consumer tile supply distributors:

  • Pre-Verify System Preference: Never buy a Wedi kit and hire a lifelong Schluter installer, or vice versa. Ask your tile setter which system they are factory-certified to install, then offer to have the complete pallet waiting in the garage on Day 1.
  • Order Complete Bundled Kits: Online distributors sell pre-packaged "Curbless Shower Kits" that bundle the pan, drain flange, curb-less transition ramps, sealant tubes, and fastener boxes at a 15% discount compared to buying individual SKUs at a retail box store.
  • Leave Thinset to the Pros: Buy the foam boards, pan, drain, and sealants yourself, but allow the contractor to supply their own bagged thinset mortar. Contractors get pallets of mortar for pennies on the dollar, and letting them supply the chemistry ensures they cannot blame homeowner-supplied mortar if a tile debonds.
