Understanding Running Watts vs. Starting Watts

The "Surge" Motor Factor

Any appliance with an electric motor (refrigerators, well pumps, AC compressors, furnace blowers) requires a massive burst of power for a split second to overcome inertia when turning on. This is called the Starting Wattage or Surge Wattage. A refrigerator that runs continuously at 700 watts may need up to 2,200 watts just to click on.

How the Math Works

You do not need to add the surge wattage of every appliance together, because you will not start them all at the exact same millisecond. To properly size a generator:

  1. Add up the Running Watts of every appliance you want to run at the same time.
  2. Find the appliance with the Highest Starting Wattage.
  3. Add that single highest surge number to your running watts total.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use a portable generator to power my whole house?

Yes, but you must have a licensed electrician install a manual transfer switch or an interlock kit. This legally and safely connects the generator to your breaker panel while automatically disconnecting your house from the grid so you don't electrocute utility workers.

Are solar generators (power stations) good for home backup?

Solar generators (like EcoFlow or Bluetti) are excellent for short-term outages. They are silent, emit no carbon monoxide (meaning they can sit inside your living room), and require no maintenance. However, unless you have thousands of watts of solar panels to recharge them, their batteries will deplete rapidly if running heavy loads like AC or well pumps.
