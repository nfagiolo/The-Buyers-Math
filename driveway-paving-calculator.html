Driveway Paving Cost & Material Guide (2026)

Installing or resurfacing a driveway significantly enhances curb appeal and protects vehicles. Choosing between asphalt, poured concrete, and interlocking pavers requires evaluating regional frost depths and long-term maintenance preferences.

Material Comparison Matrix

  • Asphalt: Best upfront cost ($4–$5.50/sq ft). Flexible in freezing northern climates, but requires sealcoating every 3–4 years. Lifespan: 15–20 years.
  • Concrete: Excellent durability ($8–$10/sq ft). Rigid, clean aesthetic, low maintenance, but susceptible to salt spalling in severe winter freeze-thaw cycles. Lifespan: 25–30 years.
  • Interlocking Pavers: Premium aesthetic ($18–$25/sq ft). Completely resists cracking by shifting with soil movement. Damaged pavers can be replaced individually. Lifespan: 40+ years.

Frequently Asked Questions

How thick should a residential concrete driveway be?

Standard residential driveways require a minimum of 4 inches of 4,000 PSI concrete over a compacted 4-inch gravel base. Heavy-duty vehicle parking areas should use 5 to 6 inches with steel rebar reinforcement.

How long before I can park on a newly paved driveway?

Stay off new asphalt with vehicles for at least 3 to 5 days (longer in hot summer weather). Poured concrete driveways require at least 7 full days to cure before vehicle traffic.
