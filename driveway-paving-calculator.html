Understanding Your Driveway Paving Quote

A newly paved driveway provides clean curb appeal and smooth winter snow removal. However, driveway longevity is entirely determined by sub-surface preparation: a paved surface is only as durable as the compacted aggregate gravel base beneath it.

4 Paving Contractor Red Flags to Watch For

  • The 'Leftover Asphalt from a Highway Job' Scam: Unsolicited door-to-door crews offering steep discounts on leftover asphalt are a notorious home improvement scam. Asphalt must be laid at a minimum temperature of 275°F to 300°F. Cooled asphalt cannot be compacted and disintegrates within months.
  • Laying Asphalt Directly Over Dirt or Thin Sub-Base: Paving over bare soil without a minimum of 4 to 6 inches of crushed quarry stone (21A / crusher run) results in immediate tire rutting, frost heaves, and sunken potholes after the first heavy rain.
  • Pouring Concrete Without Rebar or Fiber Reinforcement: Unreinforced 4-inch concrete slabs crack under the wheel weight of SUVs and delivery vans. Contracts should specify #4 (1/2-inch) steel rebar tied on a 12-to-18-inch grid or engineered polypropylene fiber mesh.
  • Failing to Slope Driveways Away from Foundations: Paving crews must establish at least a 1% to 2% grade (1/8" to 1/4" per foot) sloping water away from garages and basements to prevent catastrophic foundation flooding.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long must I wait to drive on a new driveway?

Hot-mix asphalt requires 48 to 72 hours before parking vehicles on it (and takes up to 12 months to fully cure). Concrete requires at least 7 days before supporting vehicle traffic to reach sufficient compressive strength.

How often should an asphalt driveway be sealcoated?

New asphalt should cure for 6 to 12 months before its first sealcoat application. Thereafter, commercial-grade coal-tar or asphalt-emulsion sealcoating every 3 to 4 years prevents UV oxidation and fuel spill degradation.
