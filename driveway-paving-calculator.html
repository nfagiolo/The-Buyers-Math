The 2026 Guide to Driveway Materials and Costs

Upgrading your driveway improves curb appeal and provides a solid return on investment. In 2026, homeowners typically spend between $4,500 and $12,000 to pave a standard two-car driveway, heavily dependent on the chosen material and local labor rates.

Asphalt vs. Concrete

Asphalt is the most common driveway surface in colder climates because it flexes with freeze-thaw cycles without cracking severely, and melts snow faster due to its dark color. At an average of $8 per square foot, it is more affordable upfront but requires sealcoating every 3 to 5 years.

Concrete is favored in warmer climates. It costs more initially—around $11 per square foot—but boasts a longer lifespan of up to 40 years with minimal maintenance. It can also be stamped and stained to mimic the look of premium stone or brick.

The Hidden Cost: Base Preparation

A driveway is only as good as the ground beneath it. If your existing base is damaged or if you are paving over raw dirt, contractors must excavate and compact 4 to 8 inches of gravel sub-base. This critical step adds $1 to $2 per square foot to the total project cost. If an old driveway needs to be demolished and hauled away, expect an additional $1 to $3 per square foot.
