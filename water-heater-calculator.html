Understanding Your Water Heater Replacement Quote

Water heaters account for roughly 18% of a typical home's total utility bills—second only to space heating and cooling. When replacing an aging or leaking water heater, homeowners must weigh upfront replacement cost against ongoing annual fuel operating expenses.

4 Water Heater Contractor Red Flags to Watch For

  • Quoting a Gas Tankless Unit Without Verifying Gas Pipe Sizing: Condensing tankless water heaters require 199,000 BTUs of gas—roughly 5x the gas volume of a standard 40,000 BTU tank. Running a tankless unit on a 1/2-inch gas pipe starves both your furnace and water heater. A 3/4" or 1" gas line is mandatory.
  • Omitting the Thermal Expansion Tank: When water is heated, it expands in volume. In closed municipal water systems equipped with backflow check valves, thermal expansion spikes water pressure to 120+ PSI, blowing out tank seams or failing plumbing fixtures. Expansion tanks are required by modern IPC plumbing codes.
  • Charging $3,500+ for a Routine 50-Gallon Atmospheric Tank: Unless gas lines must be completely re-routed or masonry chimneys relined, a standard 50-gallon atmospheric gas or electric water heater replacement should cost $1,700 to $2,400 turnkey. Quotes exceeding $3,000 often indicate predatory commission-based plumbing franchises.
  • Skipping the Drain Pan and Automatic Flood Sensor in Finished Areas: Placing a water heater in an interior closet, second floor, or finished basement without an aluminum drain pan plumbed to a floor drain risks catastrophic flood damage when the tank eventually rusts through.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does a water heater last?

Standard gas and electric tank water heaters last 8 to 12 years. Hybrid heat pump water heaters last 10 to 15 years, while high-efficiency gas tankless units last 20+ years with regular annual descaling.

Can I replace a gas water heater with a hybrid heat pump?

Yes. A licensed electrician will cap the gas line and run a dedicated 240V 30A electrical circuit to the utility room. The $2,000 federal Section 25C tax credit and $350+/year electricity savings typically pay for the conversion in under 3 years.
