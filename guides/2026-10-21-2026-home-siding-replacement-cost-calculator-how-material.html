Understanding the Full Cost Framework

Material Unit Costs in 2026

Material pricing for exterior siding in 2026 reflects ongoing supply chain normalization following post-pandemic volatility, though aluminum and steel products remain elevated due to domestic tariff structures. The following unit costs represent mid-range quality tiers for a typical 2,000-square-foot single-story home requiring approximately 1,500 square feet of net siding surface after accounting for doors, windows, and trim deductions:

  • Vinyl Siding: $1.80–$3.50 per square foot for materials; double-4 and Dutch-lap profiles trending toward $2.40 average in 2026.
  • Fiber-Cement (e.g., James Hardie HardiePlank): $3.50–$6.00 per square foot for materials; factory-primed planks averaging $4.25 per square foot.
  • Engineered Wood (e.g., LP SmartSide): $2.75–$4.80 per square foot for materials; strand-composite panels averaging $3.60 per square foot.
  • Steel Siding: $5.50–$9.00 per square foot for materials; 29-gauge insulated profiles averaging $7.10 per square foot.
  • Natural Cedar or Redwood: $4.00–$8.50 per square foot for materials; vertical tongue-and-groove averaging $6.20 per square foot.
  • Brick Veneer: $9.00–$15.00 per square foot for materials; standard modular units averaging $11.40 per square foot.

Licensed Trade Labor Rates in 2026

Labor costs account for 40–60% of total installed price depending on material complexity. The Bureau of Labor Statistics Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics program places the 2025 median wage for construction and extraction occupations at $27.84 per hour nationally, with licensed exterior cladding installers billing $58–$95 per hour in high-cost metros (New York, San Francisco, Boston) and $42–$68 per hour in mid-tier markets (Dallas, Phoenix, Charlotte). Fiber-cement installation requires additional labor for cutting, dust management (respirable silica compliance under OSHA 1926.1153), and painting, adding $1.50–$2.80 per square foot beyond vinyl labor benchmarks. Brick veneer masonry demands licensed masons at $75–$120 per hour, representing the highest labor-cost category.

IRC and NEC Building Code Compliance Costs

The 2024 IRC, adopted by most jurisdictions effective 2025–2026, introduces several siding-relevant provisions that affect installed cost. IRC Section R703.1.1 now mandates a continuous water-resistive barrier (WRB) behind all cladding types, with a drainage gap of at least 3/16 inch required in climate zones 4 through 8. Installing a code-compliant WRB such as Huber ZIP System or Henry BlueskinVP100 adds $0.60–$1.20 per square foot to total project cost.

Where electrical service panels or exterior lighting fixtures must be relocated during siding work, NEC 2023 Article 230 requires service entrance conductors to maintain minimum clearance from combustible cladding surfaces. This commonly triggers licensed electrician involvement at $95–$145 per hour. Homeowners should budget $800–$2,400 for electrical compliance work on older homes undergoing full siding replacement. Permit fees typically range from $350 to $1,800 depending on jurisdiction and project scope, and should never be omitted, as unpermitted siding work can void homeowner insurance claims and complicate property sales.

IRA Tax Credits Applicable to Siding Projects in 2026

Section 25C: Energy-Efficient Home Improvement Credit

Under IRA Section 25C as modified through 2032, homeowners may claim a 30% nonrefundable tax credit (maximum $1,200 per year) for exterior insulation installed as part of a siding replacement project. Rigid foam insulation board meeting IRC Table N1102.1.3 R-value thresholds—R-5 minimum in climate zones 4–8 installed beneath new cladding—qualifies when product certification under ENERGY STAR requirements is maintained. The credit applies to insulation materials cost only, not labor. A homeowner installing $4,200 of qualifying rigid foam insulation could receive a $1,200 credit, reducing effective insulation cost by approximately 28.6%.

Section 30C and 25D Intersections

Section 25D's 30% Residential Clean Energy Credit does not directly apply to siding materials but becomes relevant when siding replacement is performed concurrently with solar panel installation requiring roof or wall penetrations. Section 30C's Alternative Fuel Vehicle Refueling Property Credit similarly intersects when EV conduit is run through exterior walls during siding work. Consult a CPA for bundled project tax strategy, as IRS Notice 2023-29 guidance on energy communities may provide bonus credit percentages for qualifying census tracts.

Maximizing Long-Term Value: Decision Framework

When Fiber-Cement Wins

Homeowners planning to sell within 10–15 years in mid-to-upper housing market segments will consistently achieve the best risk-adjusted return with fiber-cement. Its 76% cost-recoup rate, code-accepted fire resistance (IRC R302.1 noncombustibility compliance pathway), and minimal deferred-maintenance risk make it the de facto benchmark material in 2026 contractor recommendations.

When Steel Justifies Its Premium

In coastal, high-wind (ASCE 7-22 exposure category C or D), or wildfire-interface zones, steel siding's 40-to-75-year lifespan and Class A fire rating eliminate repetitive replacement cycles that erode vinyl's apparent cost advantage. Homeowners remaining in place for 20+ years will find steel's 30-year TCO per square foot competitive with fiber-cement when maintenance savings are discounted at a 3% real rate.

Contractor Vetting and Project Sequencing

Always verify that contractors hold state-issued general contractor or specialty contractor licenses, carry minimum $1 million general liability insurance, and pull permits independently. Request written confirmation of OSHA silica compliance plans when fiber-cement cutting is involved. Sequencing insulation installation before cladding—and preserving manufacturer certification documents—is essential to substantiate IRS Form 5695 claims for the Section 25C credit at tax time.
