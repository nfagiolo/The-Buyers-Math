2026 Material Unit Costs: Benchmarked by Category

Material costs in 2026 reflect continued pressure from domestic manufacturing capacity constraints, tariff schedules on imported aluminum and vinyl composites, and elevated transportation costs. The following per-square-foot installed material costs (excluding labor) represent national median contractor purchase pricing, not retail MSRP:

  • Vinyl Siding (standard lap, .040" thickness): $1.85–$3.20 per sq ft
  • Fiber Cement (HardiePlank equivalent, 8.25" exposure): $3.60–$6.10 per sq ft
  • Engineered Wood Composite (LP SmartSide or equivalent): $3.10–$5.40 per sq ft
  • Insulated Vinyl (foam-backed, R-2.5 to R-4.0 rating): $4.20–$7.80 per sq ft
  • Aluminum Siding (.019" residential grade): $3.40–$5.90 per sq ft
  • Cedar or Redwood Natural Wood (clear grade, beveled): $6.80–$12.50 per sq ft
  • Stone Veneer (manufactured composite panels): $9.50–$18.40 per sq ft
  • Stucco (three-coat traditional system): $7.20–$14.00 per sq ft
  • Insulated Metal Panel (commercial-grade residential): $11.00–$22.00 per sq ft

For a 2,000 sq ft single-story home with approximately 1,400 sq ft of net wall surface area (accounting for window and door openings), total material expenditure ranges from $2,590 for standard vinyl to $25,760 for manufactured stone veneer before labor and code compliance items are added.

Licensed Trade Labor Rates in 2026

Siding installation requires licensed general contractors or specialty exterior cladding contractors in 38 states as of 2026. Prevailing wage rates have risen 11–17% since 2023 across most major metros due to persistent skilled trades shortages. Benchmark labor rates per square foot of installed wall surface include:

  • Vinyl installation: $2.10–$3.80 per sq ft (journeyman carpenter rate: $38–$58/hr)
  • Fiber cement: $3.90–$6.50 per sq ft (requires dust containment; HardiePlank installation certification recommended)
  • Engineered wood: $3.20–$5.60 per sq ft
  • Stucco (three-coat): $8.00–$16.00 per sq ft (licensed plasterer rate: $62–$94/hr)
  • Stone veneer: $12.00–$24.00 per sq ft (licensed masonry contractor)
  • Tear-off and disposal of existing siding: $1.20–$2.40 per sq ft flat rate

Permit fees vary by jurisdiction but average $380–$950 for a full exterior re-side, with structural inspection fees adding $150–$400 where required.

IRC and NEC Building Code Compliance Requirements

The 2024 International Residential Code (IRC), adopted with state amendments effective through 2026 in most jurisdictions, establishes critical compliance requirements that directly affect siding replacement costs. Section R703 governs exterior wall coverings and mandates:

  • A minimum 15-lb felt or approved housewrap weather-resistive barrier (WRB) beneath all cladding systems. ZIP System sheathing qualifies as an integrated WRB. Housewrap cost: $0.18–$0.45 per sq ft installed.
  • Continuous drainage plane with a minimum 1/8" air gap behind fiber cement and wood-based products per IRC R703.2.
  • Flashing at all penetrations, windows, doors, and roof-to-wall intersections per IRC R703.4. Budget $380–$1,200 for aluminum or copper step flashing replacement during a full re-side.
  • IRC Section R602.3 requires that fastening schedules for siding be coordinated with shear wall design; this may trigger engineered drawings in high-wind or seismic zones, adding $600–$2,400 to project costs.

NEC Article 225 governs exterior branch circuits and requires that any electrical service entries disturbed during re-siding be restored to current weatherproof standards. Conduit and weatherhead restoration averages $280–$740 depending on service configuration and utility requirements.

IRA Section 25C, 30C, and 25D Tax Credits Applicable to Siding Projects

The Inflation Reduction Act's residential energy credit provisions continue to offer meaningful offsets for qualifying siding upgrades through 2026. Homeowners should note the following credit eligibility rules for the 2026 tax year:

  • IRA Section 25C (Energy Efficient Home Improvement Credit): Insulated siding products meeting or exceeding IECC 2021 climate zone R-value thresholds qualify for a 30% credit on installed costs, up to an annual cap of $1,200. Insulated vinyl with R-3.0 or greater foam backing is the most commonly qualifying product category. The credit is nonrefundable and claimed on IRS Form 5695.
  • Section 25C Envelope Component Cap: The $1,200 annual cap is shared across exterior doors, windows, skylights, and insulation/air sealing improvements, meaning homeowners must strategically sequence projects across tax years to maximize capture.
  • Section 30C (Alternative Fuel Vehicle Refueling Property): Not directly applicable to siding, but contractors integrating EV conduit rough-in during exterior work may bundle costs for separate credit analysis.
  • Section 25D (Residential Clean Energy Credit): Applicable where integrated photovoltaic siding panels (BIPV) are installed. BIPV siding systems qualify for the full 30% uncapped residential solar credit. Representative BIPV product costs range from $22–$48 per sq ft installed in 2026.

Appraisal Equity Gains: Cost-Value Analysis by Material

Remodeling Magazine's 2026 Cost vs. Value Report and Fannie Mae appraisal guidance both indicate that exterior cladding condition and material quality are first-order factors in comparable sales adjustments. Empirical resale value recovery rates by material category:

  • Fiber cement: 76–84% cost recoupment at resale; strongest performer in most markets
  • Insulated vinyl: 68–76% cost recoupment; energy efficiency narrative supports appraiser adjustments
  • Engineered wood: 70–78% cost recoupment; strong in markets favoring natural aesthetics
  • Standard vinyl: 62–70% cost recoupment; functional but perceived as entry-level
  • Stone veneer (accent application): 81–92% recoupment on accent applications under 300 sq ft
  • Stucco (new application in stucco-dominant markets): 74–88% recoupment regionally; poor performance in non-stucco markets
