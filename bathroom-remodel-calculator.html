Bathroom Remodeling: 2026 Cost Planning Guide

A well-planned bathroom renovation consistently yields one of the highest returns on investment in residential real estate, recovering between 60% and 72% of project costs upon resale. However, because bathrooms concentrate plumbing, high-voltage electrical, and waterproof tile within tight square footage, labor costs frequently exceed material purchases.

Managing Wet-Area Waterproofing Costs

The single most catastrophic failure in bathroom remodeling is water penetration behind tile walls. Never skimp on continuous waterproofing membranes (such as Schluter-KERDI or liquid-applied membranes) beneath porcelain or ceramic tiles. Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a standard 5x8 bathroom remodel cost in 2026?

A standard 40 sq ft (5x8 ft) full bathroom remodel costs between $10,500 and $18,000 for a midrange pull-and-replace, and $22,000 to $32,000 for a complete custom gut renovation with relocated plumbing rough-ins.

What percentage of a bathroom remodel budget goes toward labor?

Labor typically accounts for 50% to 60% of total bathroom remodeling expenses due to the dense concentration of licensed trades required in a small footprint (plumbers, electricians, tile setters, and drywall tapers).

Why is a 15% contingency buffer essential for bathroom remodels?

Demolishing existing tubs and tile frequently exposes subfloor water rot, outdated galvanized plumbing, or improper venting behind walls, which necessitates immediate remediation before new finishes can be installed.

📋 Contractor Vetting Checklist: 4 Questions to Ask Before Signing

Protect your home from leaks and costly change orders by verifying these technical specifications:

✓ Specify Continuous Shower Waterproofing

Require cement backer board with taped seams and a certified waterproofing membrane (e.g., Schluter Kerdi or RedGard) tested before tile setting.

✓ Perform a 24-Hour Shower Pan Flood Test

Insist on a 24-hour standing water flood test of the shower pan liner before tiling to prove zero leaks into ceilings below.

✓ Require Dedicated 20-Amp GFCI Electrical

Bathrooms require dedicated 20-amp circuits for hair dryers and heat lamps. Confirm the contractor pulls an electrical permit.

✓ Verify Exhaust Fan Ducting to Exterior

Contractors must vent high-CFM bath fans directly through the roof or exterior sidewall, never dumping moist mold-causing air into the attic.
