4. Building Code & Permit Fast-Brief: NEC 210.8(A) & (F) - GFCI & Outdoor HVAC Protection Permit Standards

GFCI and Surge Protection for Kitchens, Bathrooms, and Outdoor Heat Pumps

The National Electrical Code requires Ground-Fault Circuit-Interrupter (GFCI) protection on all single-phase receptacles 125V through 250V in kitchens, bathrooms, unfinished basements, and outdoor locations. Furthermore, outdoor HVAC equipment (including mini-split and heat pump condensing units) requires readily accessible GFCI and Type 1 or Type 2 Surge Protective Devices (NEC 242.66).

Is a Municipal Building Permit Required?

Yes. Any panel alteration, branch circuit addition, or condensing unit disconnect replacement triggers an electrical permit and rough/final municipal inspection.

Municipal Inspector Rough/Final Checklist

  • ✓ Dual-function AFCI/GFCI breakers tested and mapped in panel schedule.
  • ✓ Outdoor disconnect switch located within sight and readily accessible from condensing unit (NEC 440.14).
  • ✓ Surge protective device installed with leads as short and straight as possible.
