Understanding Your Attic Insulation Quote

Upgrading your attic to DOE R-49 or R-60 standards is universally recognized as the single highest-ROI energy efficiency improvement you can make to an existing home—delivering a 15% to 25% annual return in heating and cooling bill reductions.

4 Insulation Contractor Red Flags to Watch For

  • Blowing Insulation Without Pre-Sealing Air Bypasses: Blowing loose cellulose over open electrical wire holes, unsealed plumbing chases, and recessed lights is a waste of money. Warm air effortlessly bypasses loose insulation, carrying moisture that can rot the underside of your roof deck.
  • Failing to Install Rafter Baffles at the Eaves: Blowing insulation directly against the exterior eaves blocks critical soffit intake airflow. Every rafter bay must receive an engineered cardboard or foam baffle (AccuVent) to maintain airflow into the ridge vents.
  • Spraying Foam Over Live Knob-and-Tube or Unrated Cans: Spraying closed-cell or open-cell polyurethane foam over older wiring or non-IC rated recessed lighting is an extreme fire hazard. All electrical fixtures must be verified or sealed in fire-rated insulated covers.
  • Demanding Unnecessary $3,000 Full Insulation Vacuum Removal: Unless previous insulation has suffered extensive rodent infestation, severe mold growth, or asbestos contamination (e.g. zonolite vermiculite), clean, dry existing fiberglass or rockwool can safely be capped with blown cellulose.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which is better for attics: blown cellulose or blown fiberglass?

Blown cellulose offers higher density (R-3.5 to R-3.8 per inch vs R-2.5 for loose fiberglass), blocks convective airflow significantly better, does not itch, and is treated with borates to repel insects and rodents.

What R-value does the Department of Energy recommend for attics?

The DOE recommends R-38 to R-49 for southern climate zones (Zones 1–3) and R-49 to R-60 for northern and central climate zones (Zones 4–7), equivalent to roughly 14 to 18 inches of dense blown cellulose.
