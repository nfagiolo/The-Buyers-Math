Why Thermal Break Subfloor Membranes Cut Radiant Heating Bills by 35%

Installing electric heating cables directly onto an uninsulated concrete basement slab or over an unconditioned crawlspace forces the slab to act as a massive downward heat sink—increasing warm-up lag times from 25 minutes to over 2 hours and raising monthly kWh consumption by 35% to 50%. Specifying an uncoupling membrane with an integrated polypropylene thermal break fleece (such as Schluter Ditra-Heat-DUO) or 1/4-inch extruded polystyrene (XPS) backer board reflects radiant heat upward into the porcelain tile, allowing programmable setback schedules to reach 82°F floor surface temperature in under 30 minutes.

Why must my installer bury two floor temperature sensors in the thinset?

A backup thermistor sensor wire costs under $25 and sits unconnected inside the thermostat junction box; if the primary sensor ever fails years later, the electrician can swap the low-voltage leads in 5 minutes without chiseling up a single tile.
