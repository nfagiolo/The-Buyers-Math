Understanding Your Home Siding Quote

Replacing exterior siding yields one of the highest curb appeal and resale equity returns in residential remodeling—typically recovering 68% to 84% on resale. However, poor installation details like over-tight nailing or missing moisture kick-out flashings can trap water and destroy wall sheathing.

4 Siding Contractor Red Flags to Watch For

  • Face-Nailing Vinyl Siding Too Tightly: Vinyl panels expand and contract significantly across seasonal temperatures. Fasteners must be driven into the center of the nailing slots with a 1/32" gap beneath the nail head. Nailing tight to the wall causes severe ripples and buckling.
  • Omitting Step and Kick-Out Flashing: Where a sloped roof meets a vertical sided wall, water cascading down the roof must be diverted into the gutter by a kick-out diverter flashing. Without it, rainwater funnels straight behind the siding into wall cavities.
  • Tearing Off Siding Without Inspecting Sheathing: Contracts should specify an agreed per-sheet price ($85–$125) for replacing rotted OSB/plywood sheathing discovered during tear-off, preventing extortionate surprise change orders.
  • Using Untreated Raw Wood Siding Trim: Fiber cement and engineered wood siding require rot-proof composite, pre-primed engineered trim, or PVC trim boards at exterior corners and window surrounds to maintain manufacturer warranty coverage.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is fiber cement (James Hardie) worth the extra cost over vinyl?

Fiber cement costs roughly 40% to 55% more than standard vinyl installed, but it delivers non-combustible Class A fire resistance, withstands hail impacts without cracking, mimics authentic wood lap siding, and yields an 80%+ resale recovery.

How long does it take a crew to re-side a standard home?

An experienced 3-to-4 person crew typically completes tear-off, weather barrier installation, and siding hanging on a 2,000 sq ft home in 5 to 8 working days, weather permitting.
