Why You Shouldn't "Oversize" Your AC

Bigger is Not Always Better in HVAC

Many homeowners assume buying the largest BTU air conditioner will cool the room faster and better. However, an oversized unit will short-cycle. It drops the room temperature rapidly and shuts off before it has time to pass enough air over the evaporator coils to remove humidity. The result is a room that feels "cold but clammy," alongside massive spikes in your electric bill due to constant compressor starts.

Understanding Tons vs. BTUs

In the HVAC industry, air conditioning capacity is measured in both BTUs (British Thermal Units) and "Tons". A ton has nothing to do with the physical weight of the machine; it originates from the amount of heat required to melt one ton of ice over 24 hours.

  • 9,000 BTU = 0.75 Ton (Best for small bedrooms)
  • 12,000 BTU = 1.0 Ton (Best for standard living rooms)
  • 18,000 BTU = 1.5 Ton (Best for large master suites / small garages)
  • 24,000 BTU = 2.0 Ton (Best for 2-car garages / open basements)
  • 36,000 BTU = 3.0 Ton (Best for open concept floors / large workshops)

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I install a MrCool DIY system myself?

Yes. The defining feature of "DIY" branded mini-splits is their pre-charged line sets. Standard mini-splits require an EPA-certified technician to braze copper lines, pressure test with nitrogen, and pull a vacuum. DIY lines have specialized quick-connect valves that release the refrigerant when tightened. You will, however, need to drill a 3.5" hole through your wall and safely wire the electrical disconnect.

Why does a kitchen require more BTUs?

Kitchens generate massive amounts of internal heat load from ovens, stovetops, dishwashers, and refrigerators exhausting heat. Standard sizing guidelines recommend adding an additional 4,000 BTUs to whatever the square-footage calculation dictates to compensate for appliance heat.
