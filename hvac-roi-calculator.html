Heat Pump vs. Gas Furnace: 2026 Buying Guide

Choosing between an electric heat pump and a high-efficiency gas furnace involves balancing upfront installation expenses against long-term fuel costs, dual heating/cooling capabilities, and available federal incentives.

Federal Incentives Under Section 25C

Qualified heat pumps that meet Consortium for Energy Efficiency (CEE) highest tier specifications are eligible for a 30% federal tax credit up to $2,000 annually, significantly offsetting the upfront equipment cost. Frequently Asked Questions

Can a heat pump replace both my furnace and central air conditioner?

Yes. A heat pump functions as an air conditioner in the summer by extracting indoor heat, and reverses in winter to extract heat from outside air, eliminating the need for a separate AC condenser.

How long do modern heat pumps last?

With annual maintenance and regular filter changes, modern inverter-driven heat pumps typically have a service life of 15 to 20 years.

📋 Contractor Vetting Checklist: 4 Questions to Ask Before Signing

Protect your home and budget by verifying these critical technical standards with prospective HVAC contractors:

✓ Require Manual J Load Calculation

Never accept rule-of-thumb square footage sizing. Insist on a formal Manual J calculation to prevent oversized equipment short-cycling.

✓ Inspect Ductwork Static Pressure

Ensure existing supply and return ducts can handle heat pump airflow (typically 400 CFM per ton).

✓ Verify Section 25C AHRI Certificate

Obtain the AHRI reference certificate proving the exact condenser and air handler combination qualifies for the $2,000 tax credit.

✓ Cold-Climate COP at 5°F

Verify the heat pump maintains a Coefficient of Performance (COP) > 1.75 at 5°F to minimize electric auxiliary heat strip usage.
