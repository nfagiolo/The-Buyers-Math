Variable-Speed Pool Pump Energy Guide (2026)

A traditional single-speed pool pump is often the second largest electricity consumer in a residential home, out-consumed only by central air conditioning. Department of Energy (DOE) standards mandate variable-speed technology due to pump Affinity Laws.

The Affinity Law Explained

According to pump Affinity Laws, cutting a pump motor's speed (RPM) in half reduces power consumption by a factor of eight (cubed relationship). Running a pump at low speed for 12 hours circulates more water with up to 75% less electricity than running at full speed for 6 hours.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are single-speed pool pumps still legal to sell?

Federal DOE regulations prohibit manufacturers from producing residential filtration pumps larger than 1.0 total horsepower that do not meet strict Energy Star weighted energy factor (WEF) standards, effectively phasing out single-speed pumps.

How quiet are variable-speed pumps?

Running at low filtration speeds (1,200 to 1,800 RPM), variable-speed pumps generate under 45 decibels—roughly the volume of a quiet library whisper.
